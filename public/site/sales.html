Where you left the line

The moments the coach would have handled differently. Tap a time to hear it.

QUESTIONS
Client said

What do you want to talk about the website?

Rep said

Well, I see that your website’s built on WordPress. And I don’t know if you, have you seen the news lately?

Try instead

“I noticed the site is on WordPress. Before I assume anything, what does the site mainly do for you today, leads, bookings, payments, or just information?”

This was the right moment to gather intelligence and qualify fit instead of steering the client into a generic news hook.

ETHICS
Client said

No.

Rep said

Any company that is on WordPress, their leads are not private. Their credit cards on the website are not private. Anything on the website is not private.

Try instead

“There have been some WordPress-related security concerns, usually depending on plugins and updates. I’m not saying your site is definitely exposed, but if you collect leads or payments, it’s worth checking. Do you collect customer details through the site?”

The absolute claim is pressure-heavy and weakens trust; control of the call requires authority with honesty, not fear.

CLOSING
Rep said

If you have just two minutes, I’d like to explain what I would like to do. And I believe that’s to your benefit.

Try instead

“If it turns out you’re collecting leads or payments and the setup is exposed, I can show you the safest options. First, can I ask two quick questions so I don’t waste your time?”

The rep asked to present before qualifying; a stronger close keeps the line by earning the right to explain through the client’s answers.

Do this next call

Intelligence: big-picture to specific questions

The biggest win is to stop pitching before qualifying. On this call the client was open, but the rep used the opening to talk 80% of the time instead of finding pain, impact and urgency in the client’s words.

Say this

“The reason I’m calling is I noticed the site is on WordPress. Before I assume anything, can I ask two quick questions so I can point you the right way? What does the website mainly need to do for you today, bring in leads, take payments, bookings, or something else?”

Drill: Practice a 20-second opener followed by three questions. Record yourself and do not allow any explanation longer than 15 seconds until the prospect has answered what the site does, what is not working and whether leads or payments matter.

Trust, with ethical authority

The client needs to trust the solution, the rep and Igani at the same time. Absolute scare lines create resistance; a sharp expert uses careful language, earns trust and then explains risk in the client’s context.

Say this

“I’m not saying your site is definitely exposed. What I am saying is WordPress sites can become vulnerable depending on plugins, updates and forms. If you’re collecting leads or payments, it’s worth a quick check. Are you taking any customer details through the site right now?”

Drill: Replace every absolute claim with a careful expert claim plus a question. Use “some”, “can”, “depending on” and “worth checking” until you have evidence from the client.

Ask next time

?“What does the website mainly do for you today, leads, bookings, payments, or just information?”
?“What are you happy with on the current WordPress site, and what would you change if you could?”
?“Are you collecting any customer details or payments through forms on the site?”
?“Has security, speed, maintenance or plugin updates been a headache for you at all?”
?“If there was a real issue with the site, who besides you would need to be involved in deciding what to do?”

What went well

✓Opening, first four seconds

The best moment was the reasonable micro-permission, “If you have just one minute, I’d like to talk about your website.” That kept the call alive and got a clear yes.

✓Control of the call

When the client asked, “What do you want to talk about the website?”, the rep answered directly by naming the platform he saw.
